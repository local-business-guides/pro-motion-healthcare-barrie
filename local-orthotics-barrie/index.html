What to bring when you visit

Think about the places where you spend most of your time on your feet. Shoes worn at work, around the house, or during exercise may differ in shape and purpose. Bring the pair you most want to discuss, and mention if you need to use an orthotic with more than one kind of shoe. That context helps keep the conversation connected to your usual routine.

It may also help to jot down a few details before you go:

  • Where you notice discomfort or difficulty
  • When it started and whether anything changes it
  • Activities you have cut back on or would like to return to
  • Any previous advice, treatment, or orthotic use

Keep the notes brief. A few clear examples are often easier to explain than a long list of general concerns. If your symptoms followed a sports injury, work-related issue, or motor vehicle accident, mention that when contacting the clinic. Pro Motion Healthcare works with patients dealing with these circumstances.

Avoid a rushed footwear decision

An orthotic and a shoe need to work together in practice. A device that seems suitable in one pair may not fit the footwear you rely on for work or daily errands. Before buying an insert or making changes to a current device, think about where and how you plan to use it. Ask about shoe compatibility and what to do if the fit feels wrong.

Don't assume that a recommendation someone else received will suit you too. People have different routines, footwear, and concerns. It's also worth avoiding sudden changes to how long you wear a device without asking for guidance, especially if you're noticing discomfort. Share what's happening and seek advice rather than trying to reshape an orthotic yourself.

When to call a Barrie clinic

Consider reaching out when foot or ankle problems are interfering with walking, work, or activities you enjoy. A call can help you explain the issue and ask what to prepare, even if you're still deciding whether to book. If you already have an orthotic that feels uncomfortable or no longer works with your regular shoes, describe the problem and ask about appropriate next steps.
